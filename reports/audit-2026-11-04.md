The 72 `speak` cards place the largest demand on sentence generation, though a fixed answer may become memorized. Repeating the printed answer aloud after flipping is useful pronunciation practice but is not evidence of unprompted production. More in [grammar-design.md](../docs/grammar-design.md).

## Other English decks: purpose, fit, and action

**`(Cún) 📚DESTINATION C1&C2` — ARCHIVE.** Thirteen units contain 1,505 notes and 3,010 new cards (two templates per note). It teaches advanced word meaning and example usage. The `Flashcard` shows the English word; `Suffer Quiz` uses multiple-choice and typing modes. It can strengthen word recognition or cued recall, but does not require the learner to express a self-chosen idea in context. The 3,010-card potential workload is disproportionate to the stated speaking goal. Leave it intact; draw an occasional useful word from real speaking gaps instead of starting this queue.

**`Collocations` — MERGE selectively.** 200 notes create 400 new cards, with definitions, example sentences, pronunciation, and audio. These chunks could improve natural phrasing, but its card fronts often show the target collocation, so one direction is recognition; the other `type:words` direction still displays `words` in its template. It is weak as a free-production drill. Select a few chunks that solve observed speaking problems and embed them in situation cards; do not move or delete source notes yet.

**`Essential Idioms in English` — ARCHIVE.** 468 notes/cards are new. Fronts present meaning and examples and request `type:Expression`, so this is productive *expression retrieval*, more demanding than simple recognition. The exact idiom remains a single expected answer; transfer to spontaneous speech is uncertain. Review only when an idiom is genuinely useful in your conversations. Six normalized expression values repeat within the deck, which may reflect variants or duplicate entries.

**`Hackers Speaking Phrases` — MERGE selectively.** 262 new cards over 20 units prompt with Vietnamese, a letter hint, and `type:1A_Phrase`. ## Overlap analysis

I normalized case, punctuation, and HTML and compared selected target expressions and example sentences. Exact cross-group matches were limited: 2 between `DESTINATION` and `Collocations`, 4 between `DESTINATION` and `Essential Idioms`, 1 between `Collocations` and `Sưu tầm`, and 1 between `Essential Idioms` and `Sưu tầm`. No exact target sentence match with `English Grammar` was found. This is a **lower bound**: paraphrases, synonyms, and common grammar patterns can be redundant without exact strings.

Within primary target fields, `DESTINATION` has 1,349 normalized unique values across 1,505 notes (156 repeated values), `Sưu tầm` 344 across 354 (10 repeated), and `Essential Idioms` 462 across 468 (6 repeated). Repeated words in `DESTINATION` may represent different senses; these numbers are screening signals, not automatic deletion candidates. Grammar `Key` has 581 unique values.

A staged separate profile opened in Anki 26.5 with 3,792 notes, 5,497 cards and all 1,590 review logs. A native whole-collection `.colpkg` with media was exported from that staged profile and passed ZIP CRC checks; its **import** has not been independently tested. The source already referenced one missing Hackers image, which neither backup format can invent.

The `.colpkg` archive contains 12,264 media entries; its embedded collection was checked directly and has 3,792 notes, 5,497 cards and 1,590 review rows. No live cleanup has occurred. The next reversible step, if desired, is to stop introducing new cards from archived decks while keeping their notes and review data. Moving cards to another deck can change the preset used for future scheduling; deleting cards can remove their study state and may affect history. Before any deletion, prepare an itemized deck/note/card list, confirm a fresh verified backup and a restore path, then obtain explicit approval for that exact list.
